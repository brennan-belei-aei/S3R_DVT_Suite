CW DATA ANALYSIS MAINTAINER'S GUIDE
===================================

DOCUMENT PURPOSE
----------------
This guide documents the current intended CW data-analysis workflow in which
all RISE and FALL captures are analyzed using a filtered Hilbert-envelope
signal, while retaining different filter cutoffs for different setpoint digit
ranges. The automatic far-right "ghost pulse" or false-blip detector is NOT
part of this stable version. A future implementation is still desired and is
described in the Future Development section.

This document is intended for engineers who need to run, maintain, tune, or
extend the script without changing its established behavior accidentally.


1. SCRIPT PURPOSE
-----------------
The CW data-analysis script:

1. Reads oscilloscope CSV waveform files.
2. Extracts RISE/FALL, RF frequency, and power setpoint from each filename.
3. Cleans invalid waveform samples and enforces increasing time values.
4. Converts the RF waveform into an amplitude envelope with a Hilbert
   transform.
5. Applies a setpoint-dependent low-pass filter to the Hilbert envelope.
6. Calculates RISE and FALL transition times.
7. Applies a grouped 1.5 x IQR statistical outlier filter.
8. Retains excluded measurements in the output for traceability.
9. Creates individual, summary, and trend plots.
10. Creates accepted-only grouped summary tables.
11. Produces Excel, CSV, and PowerPoint outputs.
12. Optionally compares two hardware-configuration data folders.


2. REQUIRED INPUT FILE FORMAT
-----------------------------
The expected filename formats are:

    RF_Freq_RISE{frequency_kHz}_Power{setpoint_W}W_{timestamp}.csv
    RF_Freq_FALL{frequency_kHz}_Power{setpoint_W}W_{timestamp}.csv

Examples:

    RF_Freq_RISE13560_Power500W_20260908_105011.csv
    RF_Freq_FALL12882_Power60W_20260908_105011.csv

The script reads:

    Column A: time
    Column B: voltage
    First waveform data row: Excel row 15, pandas index 14

Invalid or nonnumeric samples are removed. If time samples are out of order,
the samples are sorted. Duplicate time values are removed so the final time
array is strictly increasing.

4. SETPOINT-DEPENDENT HILBERT FILTERING
---------------------------------------
All setpoints use Hilbert-envelope timing in the current intended version.
The power setpoint selects the low-pass cutoff applied after the Hilbert
envelope is created.

Current behavior:

    Single-digit setpoints: fallback FILTER_CUTOFF_HZ, currently 2000 Hz
    Double-digit setpoints, 10 W to below 100 W: 35000 Hz
    Setpoints of 100 W and higher: 2000 Hz

Examples:

    6 W      -> 2000 Hz fallback
    10 W     -> 35000 Hz
    60 W     -> 35000 Hz
    99 W     -> 35000 Hz
    100 W    -> 2000 Hz
    500 W    -> 2000 Hz
    600 W    -> 2000 Hz
    1000 W   -> 2000 Hz
    6000 W   -> 2000 Hz

Although the global name TRIPLE_DIGIT_FILTER_CUTOFF_HZ contains the words
"triple digit," the associated condition is power >= 100 W. The setting
therefore also applies to four-digit and higher setpoints.

The script performs the envelope operation in this order:

    analytic_signal = hilbert(raw_voltage)
    envelope = abs(analytic_signal)
    filtered_envelope = filtfilt(low_pass_filter, envelope)

This is different from simply calculating abs(raw_voltage). The magnitude of
the analytic signal estimates the RF amplitude envelope, while preserving the
slow RISE/FALL behavior that the timing calculation is intended to measure.


5. FALL ANALYSIS EXCLUSION REGIONS
----------------------------------
The FALL calculation analyzes only a retained middle portion of the filtered
Hilbert envelope.

Current left exclusion:

    FALL_LEFT_EXCLUSION_FRACTION = 0.15

This removes the first 15 percent of the waveform from FALL threshold and
crossing calculations.

Current right-side behavior:

    Double-digit setpoints: 0.10 right exclusion
    Setpoints of 100 W and higher: 0.0 right exclusion

Therefore:

    Double-digit FALL setpoint:
        Retained region = 15 percent through 90 percent of the capture

    Setpoint of 100 W or higher:
        Retained region = 15 percent through 100 percent of the capture

Fraction settings use decimal fractions. Ten percent is 0.10, not 10.0.

FALL_RIGHT_EXCLUSION_FRACTION is the general fallback if a per-setpoint value
is not passed to the calculation. In ordinary processing, the value returned
by get_setpoint_processing_settings() is passed explicitly.


6. FALL NOISE-FLOOR CALCULATION
-------------------------------
After the retained FALL region is selected, the script estimates the noise
floor using a low percentile of the retained filtered Hilbert envelope.

Conceptually:

    analysis_envelope = filtered_envelope[search_start:search_end]

    v_max = maximum(analysis_envelope)

    noise_floor_v = percentile(
        analysis_envelope,
        FALL_NOISE_FLOOR_PERCENTILE
    )

The current script setting should be checked near the top of the file. Recent
iterations have used values such as 5.0 or 10.0.

Interpretation:

    5.0  means the 5th percentile of retained Hilbert-envelope samples
    10.0 means the 10th percentile of retained Hilbert-envelope samples

A percentile is used instead of the absolute minimum because one unusually
low sample could be caused by filtering behavior, noise, or a numerical
artifact. A low percentile provides a more robust estimate of the lower
settled envelope level.

Important dependency:

The retained region must be defined before the official noise floor is
calculated. Any false pulse, late rise, or Hilbert edge artifact included in
the retained region can change the percentile distribution and may affect the
calculated floor.


7. FALL V10 AND V90 CALCULATIONS
--------------------------------
The FALL thresholds are referenced from the calculated noise floor to the
maximum of the retained Hilbert envelope. They are not simply 10 percent and
90 percent of zero-to-maximum voltage.

The script calculates:

    signal_span_v = v_max - noise_floor_v

    v10 = noise_floor_v + 0.10 * signal_span_v
    v90 = noise_floor_v + 0.90 * signal_span_v

Example:

    Retained maximum = 5.0 V
    Noise floor      = 1.0 V
    Signal span      = 4.0 V

    v10 = 1.0 + 0.10 * 4.0 = 1.4 V
    v90 = 1.0 + 0.90 * 4.0 = 4.6 V

This floor-referenced approach is important when the Hilbert envelope settles
to a nonzero value.

After v90 and v10 are calculated, the FALL routine finds a complete downward
90-percent-to-10-percent crossing pair inside the retained region. The current
stable pre-ghost-pulse version uses the established left-to-right crossing-pair
selection. Avoid changing the crossing direction without testing real carrier
and envelope behavior carefully.

The FALL time is:

    fall_time = t10 - t90

A result is rejected if no complete downward v90-to-v10 pair is found or if
the calculated time is invalid.


8. RISE-TIME CALCULATION
------------------------
All RISE captures use the filtered Hilbert envelope in the current intended
version.

The RISE routine estimates an initial envelope level and a final envelope
level. The exact window sizes are defined in calculate_low_power_rise_time().
The established implementation:

1. Starts the initial averaging region after a small beginning offset.
2. Uses up to 500 samples for the initial-level average.
3. Uses up to 500 samples at the end for the final-level average.
4. Calculates the positive envelope span from initial to final.
5. Defines baseline-referenced v10 and v90.
6. Finds an upward 10-percent crossing followed by an upward 90-percent
   crossing.

Conceptually:

    v_initial = mean(initial_envelope_window)
    v_final = mean(final_envelope_window)

    signal_span = v_final - v_initial

    v10 = v_initial + 0.10 * signal_span
    v90 = v_initial + 0.90 * signal_span

    rise_time = t90 - t10

The routine requires a positive final-minus-initial span. It rejects captures
that do not contain a complete valid upward 10-percent-to-90-percent pair.

The current RISE implementation uses discrete crossing sample locations. If
future accuracy improvements are desired, linear crossing interpolation can
be added, but the change should be applied consistently and regression-tested
against known captures.


9. SMALL_SETPOINT_THRESHOLD
---------------------------
In the current Hilbert-at-all-setpoints version:

    SMALL_SETPOINT_THRESHOLD = 500

no longer controls whether the Hilbert transform is used. All setpoints use
Hilbert-envelope timing.

The threshold still controls the additional mixed-center trend plot:

    At or below 500 W: plot the accepted median
    Above 500 W: plot the accepted average

The original all-average trend graph is still generated separately.

A clearer future variable name would be:

    MEDIAN_TREND_SETPOINT_THRESHOLD_W = 500

Renaming should only be done after updating every reference and running the
full regression tests.


10. IQR OUTLIER FILTERING
-------------------------
The IQR filter is applied separately for each complete frequency and power
condition within RISE and FALL data.

For groups with at least MIN_SAMPLES_FOR_IQR samples:

    Q1 = 25th percentile
    Q3 = 75th percentile
    IQR = Q3 - Q1

    lower_bound = Q1 - IQR_MULTIPLIER * IQR
    upper_bound = Q3 + IQR_MULTIPLIER * IQR

With IQR_MULTIPLIER = 1.5, measurements outside these bounds receive:

    Use In Average = False

Excluded rows are retained in the workbook with an Outlier Reason. Accepted
rows alone feed summary averages, quartiles, min/max values, standard
deviations, trend plots, and PowerPoint tables.


11. AVERAGE, MEDIAN, BOX, AND WHISKER OUTPUTS
---------------------------------------------
The original trend graphs plot accepted averages at every setpoint.

The additional trend graphs plot:

    Accepted median at or below SMALL_SETPOINT_THRESHOLD
    Accepted average above SMALL_SETPOINT_THRESHOLD

IQR box:

    Bottom = Q1
    Center line = median
    Top = Q3

Whiskers:

    Lower = accepted minimum
    Upper = accepted maximum

The user may display neither, either, or both.


12. GLOBAL VARIABLES AND HOW TO TUNE THE SCRIPT
-----------------------------------------------
Always change one variable at a time and compare output against known-good
captures.

12.1 Core processing controls

SMALL_SETPOINT_THRESHOLD
    Controls where the mixed median/average trend plot switches center
    statistic. It no longer controls Hilbert use.

MIN_SAMPLES_FOR_IQR
    Minimum number of samples needed before IQR filtering applies. Increasing
    this value causes more small groups to bypass statistical filtering.

IQR_MULTIPLIER
    Controls outlier strictness. Lower values exclude more measurements.
    Higher values retain more measurements. Do not change this merely to force
    a lower standard deviation.

12.2 Hilbert filtering controls

DEFAULT_FILTER_CUTOFF_HZ
    Fallback cutoff for setpoints not covered by the digit-range rules.

DOUBLE_DIGIT_FILTER_CUTOFF_HZ
    Used for setpoints from 10 W through less than 100 W. A higher cutoff
    follows faster envelope changes but retains more envelope ripple. A lower
    cutoff smooths more strongly but can shift or broaden transitions.

TRIPLE_DIGIT_FILTER_CUTOFF_HZ
    Used for every setpoint of 100 W and above, including four-digit and higher
    values. The same smoothing tradeoff applies.

Every cutoff must be below half the waveform sample rate, or the Butterworth
filter design will fail.

12.3 FALL retained-region controls

FALL_LEFT_EXCLUSION_FRACTION
    Removes a fixed fraction from the beginning of every FALL waveform.
    Increase only if invalid left-edge behavior is contaminating the maximum
    or floor calculation. Excessive values can remove the true FALL edge.

DEFAULT_FALL_RIGHT_EXCLUSION_FRACTION
    General fallback right exclusion. It is normally overridden by the
    per-setpoint result.

DOUBLE_DIGIT_FALL_RIGHT_EXCLUSION_FRACTION
    Final fraction ignored for double-digit setpoints. Current value 0.10.

TRIPLE_DIGIT_FALL_RIGHT_EXCLUSION_FRACTION
    Final fraction ignored for every setpoint of 100 W and above. Current
    value 0.0. The name is historical and also applies to four-digit values.

FALL_NOISE_FLOOR_PERCENTILE
    Low percentile used for the official retained-envelope floor. Lower values
    move the floor toward the lowest envelope samples. Higher values move the
    floor upward. Changing this value changes both v10 and v90.

12.4 Trend and PowerPoint controls

RISE_TREND_Y_MIN_US / RISE_TREND_Y_MAX_US
    Default RISE trend limits. None permits automatic scaling.

FALL_TREND_Y_MIN_US / FALL_TREND_Y_MAX_US
    Default FALL trend limits.

POWERPOINT_TABLE_DECIMAL_PLACES
    Decimal places used for PowerPoint timing values. PowerPoint timing is
    always shown in microseconds.

POWERPOINT_FILENAME
    Output PowerPoint filename.

15. FUTURE DESIRED FEATURE: AUTOMATIC GHOST-PULSE DETECTION
-----------------------------------------------------------
There is still a desire to automatically detect a false far-right "ghost
pulse" or blip in a FALL Hilbert envelope and exclude the invalid region before
the official noise floor, v10, and v90 are calculated.

This feature is intentionally NOT active in the current stable version because
an earlier experimental implementation caused regressions and was reverted.

The key dependency order for a future implementation must be:

    Complete filtered Hilbert envelope
        -> detect stable flat noise-floor region
        -> detect later sharp positive departure
        -> define exact retained endpoint
        -> calculate official noise floor
        -> calculate v10 and v90
        -> calculate FALL crossing pair and fall time

The detector must not depend on v10, v90, idx10, idx90, or fall time because
those values depend on the retained endpoint and noise floor.

A promising future design is:

1. Search the latter portion of the complete Hilbert envelope.
2. Identify a sustained flat region using low local slope and low local
   variation.
3. Detect a sustained sharp positive departure from that established floor.
4. Place the retained endpoint at the start of that departure.
5. Require amplitude, slope, duration, location, and minimum-retained-data
   safeguards.
6. Initially operate in REPORT_ONLY mode.
7. Draw the proposed boundary on individual plots and record diagnostics in
   Excel before allowing automatic cropping.

Desired operating modes:

    OFF
    REPORT_ONLY
    AUTO_EXCLUDE

Suggested future diagnostics:

    Ghost Pulse Detected
    Detection Reason
    Stable Floor Start Time
    Stable Floor End Time
    Proposed Boundary Time
    Proposed Analysis End Index
    Floor Median
    Floor Robust Sigma
    Departure Peak
    Proposed Right Exclusion Fraction

For efficiency, moving slopes and variances can be calculated with cumulative
sums so the primary detector remains close to O(n) time complexity.

Validation should include labeled clean and ghost-pulse captures across every
frequency and relevant setpoint. False-positive and false-negative rates must
be reviewed before enabling automatic exclusion.


16. SAFE MAINTENANCE PRACTICES
------------------------------
1. Preserve the current working script and save changes under a new filename.
2. Never delete excluded rows from the individual-results output.
3. Keep RISE and FALL calculations and outlier groups separate.
4. Keep accepted-only behavior for summaries, plots, and PowerPoint tables.
5. Do not change several filter or threshold settings simultaneously.
6. Check the raw waveform and Hilbert envelope after cutoff changes.
7. Test double-digit, triple-digit, four-digit, and threshold-boundary values.
8. Test RISE-only, FALL-only, empty input, missing groups, and outliers.
9. Test all four box/whisker modes after plot changes.
10. Test automatic and manual vertical scaling.
11. Test PowerPoint decimal formatting and missing-data N/A behavior.
12. Test single-folder and comparison workflows.
13. Close Excel and PowerPoint outputs before rerunning to avoid file locks.

18. COMMON TROUBLESHOOTING
--------------------------
PermissionError when writing Excel or PowerPoint:
    Close the existing output file and any background Excel or PowerPoint
    process. Wait for OneDrive to release the file, then rerun.

Filter cutoff must be below Nyquist:
    Verify the waveform sample interval or reduce the applicable cutoff.

No positive envelope span:
    Inspect the Hilbert envelope, input waveform, trigger orientation, and
    selected filter cutoff.

No complete FALL v90-to-v10 pair:
    Inspect retained-region exclusions, percentile floor, filtered envelope,
    threshold levels, and transition completeness.

No valid RISE v10-to-v90 pair:
    Inspect initial/final averaging windows, envelope polarity, transition
    completeness, and smoothing cutoff.

Unexpected average or standard deviation:
    Inspect Use In Average, Outlier Reason, accepted counts, mean, median, Q1,
    Q3, minimum, and maximum for the affected condition.
